# The Richmond Planet's crossword, 1924–25

The Richmond Planet, the Black weekly of Richmond, Virginia, started a crossword on October 18,
1924 and ran one nearly every Saturday until at least August 15, 1925, on page six, its
"Tabloid Magazine". This is the Every Puzzle Project's record of that run. Part of the
[Every Puzzle Project](https://github.com/EveryPuzzleProject).

The first was announced as "the first of the popular cross-word puzzles which will now be
featured in The Planet from time to time", "arranged by Mr. Cedric C. French for this
newspaper", with an invitation: "Perhaps after having worked this one out you will want to
originate one of different design. If you do, send it into The Planet office and we will
print it." From November 22 the puzzle had a standing heading, "Try Your Luck With This Deep
One", a chatty introduction naming that week's maker, and last week's solved grid beside it.

## The run

39 pages are saved, with 38 different puzzles (October 25 reprints the first with its
answer). Five Saturdays in the span have no page saved; the solved grids on the following
pages show that at least four of them had a puzzle.

Nearly every puzzle names a different person: one maker appears twice, one puzzle has two
makers, and one came from "Great Bend, Kansas" with no name. `puzzles.tsv` has one row per
page: heading, the credit as printed, the grid's size, where the answer was seen, and notes.

## What isn't known

- **Whose puzzles these were.** At least from January 1925 they were not the Planet's
  own. The Carbon County News of Red Lodge, Montana, printed "THIS WEEK'S CROSS WORD
  PUZZLE ... by Magdalene Tuck", "a time limit test" to "be worked in not less than seven
  minutes", on 1925-01-22: the Planet's puzzle of 1925-01-24, two days earlier, in a white
  weekly two thousand miles away (read in the Library of Congress's page text,
  https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn84036285/1925-01-22/ed-1/?sp=3; the page image has not been
  compared). That fits the other signs: makers placed in Kansas, Oregon and Texas, and grid
  numbers "No. 16" (1925-02-21) and "No. 17" (02-28) where the Planet's own count would be 18
  and 19. So the Planet was taking a feature that supplied readers' puzzles to many papers.
  Which service, and whether the first puzzles ("arranged by Mr. Cedric C. French for this
  newspaper", October 1924) were also the service's, is not known. No copyright or syndicate
  line appears near any grid.
- **Who the makers were**, whichever is true.
- **When it ended.** August 15, 1925 is the last page saved, not a known end.
- **The five missing Saturdays** (`wanted.tsv`).

## Status (2026-11-19)

The pages are saved and indexed. Nothing has been read yet: the next step is the OCR harvest
([xword-ocr](https://github.com/EveryPuzzleProject/xword-ocr)), then review on the
[review site](https://blitz.xwordapp.com/review/). Clues and answers are not typed in here;
puzzle content comes from that pipeline, so nothing typed by hand gets in silently. The
credits in `puzzles.tsv` were read from the scans for this index and keep the paper's
spelling; a `[?]` marks a letter that couldn't be read.

## Images

Page images don't go in this repo. They live in the private twin `richmondplanet-scans`.
The pages are from 1924–25 and out of copyright in the US; whether to publish them is a
separate decision.
